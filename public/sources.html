Sourcing
standard.

This site exists because most EverQuest Legends reference material is classic EverQuest text in a Legends-shaped hole. The only defence against that is a standard applied without exceptions, including the inconvenient ones.

Five tiersEvery claim datedGaps published

01

The hierarchy

Tier 1 · strongest

Official patch notes

Dated, authoritative, and they override everything below them. Anything published after a wiki page’s last edit supersedes that page.

Tier 2

Structured wiki data

Infoboxes, NPC tables, item tables, coordinate records on eqlwiki. Machine-shaped fields that somebody entered from the live game.

Tier 3

Named community guides

eqprogression.com, and maintained wiki user guides such as Alanna’s Race Unlock Guide. Named authors, actively updated, dated. Reliable, but they are one person’s reading of the game.

Tier 4

Aggregators

EQL Build Forge, EQ Legends Tools. Useful for cross-checking a number against a second pair of eyes. Each carries a snapshot date; anything older than the last patch is treated as stale.

Tier 5 · marked on sight

Wiki prose

Large parts are a Project 1999 import, sometimes word for word. It describes a single-class game at fixed difficulty. It is quoted only when marked as classic, never as Legends fact.

Two systems break almost all inherited advice. Legends characters run three classes at once, and difficulty D0–D4 changes mob behaviour rather than mob level. Any line that says “you need a full group of level 50s” came from a game where neither was true, and is unreliable in both directions.
02

Known gaps

This list is expected to grow as verification deepens, not shrink.

Raids

D4 boss behaviour

No published source documents which class kits attach to which raid boss at D3 and D4. Needs combat logs.

Plane of Sky

Five class tooltips

Ranger, Rogue, Shadow Knight, Shaman and Wizard reward stat blocks are unconfirmed for Legends. The turn-ins and drop sources are current; only the stats are missing.

Dungeons

Respawn ceilings

The 28 July patch lowered maximum respawn times without publishing figures. Affected plates state the pre-patch timer as a ceiling rather than a current value.

Travel

Druid and wizard port levels

Two wiki pages disagree — 25/27 against 19/29. The Travel Guide has been shown wrong on translocators, so it is weighted lower, but the conflict is open.

Raids

Plane of Sky geometry

The zone has never been surveyed, so the Eye of Veeshan model is schematic rather than measured. A handful of /loc readings from islands 7 and 8 would fix it. The page says so in place.

Dungeons

Floor plans have no room names

The plans are read from the game’s own meshes, so they carry walls and storeys but no labels. Which chamber is which is still something you work out from the named roster.

Dungeons

Verification gates, all cleared

All ten surveys have passed all three gates, and the evidence for each is recorded on the plates page. That is not the same as complete. It means each survey has been checked against its live source and every coordinate lands somewhere a player can stand — the gaps listed here are what remains.

Help wanted

In-game confirmation

Most of these close with a screenshot or a log line. If you have one, it is worth more than another hour of reading wikis.

01Revamp status

The only zone in this set to be worked on twice. Splitpaw got the largest of the pre-launch reworks on 14 July, then a second pass on launch day.

The 14 July notes describe enemies of levels 25–28 at the entrance, ramping through five difficulty bands, with standard enemies at levels 40–42 in the deepest areas. Named enemies in each band can somewhat exceed the level of the standard population around them. New rare spawns were added along with new and improved equipment drops, and rare-spawn and equipment drop rates were substantially increased — guaranteed rare spawns in the lowest areas, tapering gradually toward the deepest, with named still appearing very frequently even at the end.

Launch day, 28 July 2026. Splitpaw appeared again in the launch patch, in a list of dungeons that had placeholders removed and maximum respawn times lowered. The other zones in that pass were The Hole, Nagafen's Lair, Lower Guk, The Warrens and Castle Mistmoore. The note closes with "more dungeon treatments will take place over time," so this list will grow.

Practically: enter in the mid-20s and there is no need to leave until the low 40s.

One caveat. Opinion on the revamp splits by playstyle. Solo players report a dense zone with named up on most laps. Groups working it at level report trains and aggro problems that Upper and Lower Guk don't have. Read the danger section before the first pull.

01Revamp status

Revamped 14 July 2026, then touched again on launch day. This is the most recently maintained low-level dungeon in the game.

The 14 July patch describes Clan Crushbone fortifying and multiplying its army while the elves of Faydark were distracted with politics — mechanically, fresh loot and new enemies across levels 4 through 22. Six named were added: Bloodgurgler, Bonefire, Chokehold, Marrowbane, Lord Darish and The Prophet, along with a whole attunable gear tier that did not exist in classic.

Launch day, 28 July 2026 — five further changes.
  • Rare monsters in Crushbone are now flagged as rare monsters.
  • Orc Scoutsman and Orc Warden respawn timers substantially reduced.
  • Chokehold is now susceptible to melee attacks — he was not before. Melee groups that bounced off him should try again.
  • Headsplitter Axe is no longer usable by Monks, Rogues or Beastlords. Older loot lists are wrong on this.
  • Spiky Splintmail's click has been shifted to Bramblecoat, and the item now has a worn graphic. Slave Trammels were also corrected to render as leg irons, several Crushbone items gained worn graphics, and ornamentation slots were added to the new model-visible drops.

ZEM 128 (171%), raised from 100 in the 23 June rebalance. The wiki infobox still shows the old value struck through beside the new one. This is the highest confirmed dungeon modifier in the series — a hair above Splitpaw's 128 (170%) — and it runs 49% above Lower Guk and Nagafen's Lair, which both sit at 86 (115%). For characters in the high 40s and 50s this is where the experience is.

Three launch-week changes that reshape this zone specifically

  • Charm is capped at 30 seconds. The 28 July patch caps loss-of-player-control spells, NPC charm included, at 30 seconds duration. The wiki's NPC table calls the elemental capturer an amazing enchanter and bard pet and the deceiver an excellent group pet, and classic guides to this zone treat charm as the defining tactic here. That advice is now obsolete: a charmed elemental is a 30-second cooldown, not a tank.tactic change
  • Chain-stunning no longer works. Stunned players and NPCs both now gain short-term immunity to further stuns that outlasts the current stun. The elemental crusader's counter — stun it at 20% to force flee mode — still works once, but it cannot be repeated back-to-back.
  • Loot rolls follow difficulty tier as of 29 July: you roll your tier or better, with a diminishing chance at higher tiers. Every drop source in section 05 is tier-gated on top of its own rate, and the raid change means killing a boss while holding a loot lockout still yields one guaranteed item from its unique table.

A 4–25 zone sharing the highest experience modifier in the game with a 39–56 zone is an unusual piece of tuning and the single strongest argument for levelling here.
  • A 6 minute 40 second zone spawn timer. Faster than Crushbone's 9:00 and roughly three times faster than Blackburrow's 22:00, though still slower than Befallen's 4:27. Combined with the ZEM, a named-clearing loop here pays unusually well for the bracket.
The zone is flagged as outdoors. The wiki calls this odd, and it is, but it means Spirit of Wolf, Harmony and other outdoor-only spells work inside the dungeon. Movement speed and pacification in a cave network is a real structural advantage no other dungeon in this series offers.
Faction runs both ways here. Killing kobolds raises standing with both the Heretics and the Erudites — the two factions that are otherwise opposed on Odus. For a character making Odus a long-term home that is a rare double, and it is why evil Erudites can stay near their home city for the first twenty levels.

01Revamp status, and a claim worth correcting

Treated on launch day, 28 July 2026 — placeholders removed and maximum respawn times lowered, alongside The Hole, Nagafen's Lair, Lower Guk, Lair of the Splitpaw and The Warrens. Every spawn percentage in section 04 is a pre-patch figure. This zone had unusually punishing rates — an avenging caitiff at 10%, a glyphed warder at 4%, a deathly usher at 8% — so it has as much to gain from the placeholder removal as any zone in the pass.
The wiki's prose calls this "arguably some of the best experience for any zone in the game." In EverQuest Legends that is no longer true. The rebalanced ZEM is 97 (129%), up slightly from 90. That puts Mistmoore below Crushbone, Befallen, Blackburrow and Najena, which all sit at 119 (159%), and far below The Hole and The Warrens at 128 (171%). It beats only Lower Guk and Nagafen's Lair at 86 (115%). The classic claim reflects pre-Legends tuning and a much older experience formula. Come here for the loot and the atmosphere, not for the rate.

The mechanic that defines the zone

The castle repopulates twice a day in game time — 06:00 and 20:00. At dawn the undead give way to dark elf nobles and musicians; at dusk the vampires and werewolves rise and replace many of the daytime creatures. Any monster still alive respawns anyway, and anything killed repops. The zone's 22-minute timer resets at both events.

Three consequences. First, invisibility is only half a solution — ordinary invisibility hides you from the daytime population, invisibility versus undead only from the night population, and the swap means the correct spell changes twice a day. Second, a camp cleared at 19:55 is full again at 20:00, with a different roster. Third, and worst: sitting gives level-1-equivalent aggro, so being seated at a repop tick can hand you the entire room.
