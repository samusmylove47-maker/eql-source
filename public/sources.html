Sourcing
standard.

This site exists because most EverQuest Legends reference material is classic EverQuest text in a Legends-shaped hole. The only defence against that is a standard applied without exceptions, including the inconvenient ones.

01

The hierarchy

Tier 1 · strongest

Official patch notes

Dated, authoritative, and they override everything below them. Anything published after a wiki page’s last edit supersedes that page.

Tier 2

Structured wiki data

Infoboxes, NPC tables, item tables, coordinate records on eqlwiki. Machine-shaped fields that somebody entered from the live game.

Tier 3

Named community guides

eqprogression.com, and maintained wiki user guides such as Alanna’s Race Unlock Guide. Named authors, actively updated, dated. Reliable, but they are one person’s reading of the game.

Tier 4

Aggregators

EQL Build Forge, EQ Legends Tools. Useful for cross-checking a number against a second pair of eyes. Each carries a snapshot date; anything older than the last patch is treated as stale.

Tier 5 · marked on sight

Wiki prose

Large parts are a Project 1999 import, sometimes word for word. It describes a single-class game at fixed difficulty. It is quoted only when marked as classic, never as Legends fact.

Two systems break almost all inherited advice. Legends characters run three classes at once, and difficulty D0–D4 changes mob behaviour rather than mob level. Any line that says “you need a full group of level 50s” came from a game where neither was true, and is unreliable in both directions.
02

Known gaps

This list is expected to grow as verification deepens, not shrink.

Raids

D4 boss behaviour

No published source documents which class kits attach to which raid boss at D3 and D4. Needs combat logs.

Plane of Sky

Five class tooltips

Ranger, Rogue, Shadow Knight, Shaman and Wizard reward stat blocks are unconfirmed for Legends. The turn-ins and drop sources are current; only the stats are missing.

Dungeons

Respawn ceilings

The 28 July patch lowered maximum respawn times without publishing figures. Affected plates state the pre-patch timer as a ceiling rather than a current value.

Dungeons

Placeholder removals

If placeholders are gone entirely, published spawn percentages may no longer mean anything. Unresolved, and it affects how every named roster should be written.

Travel

Druid and wizard port levels

Two wiki pages disagree — 25/27 against 19/29. The Travel Guide has been shown wrong on translocators, so it is weighted lower, but the conflict is open.

Raids

Plane of Sky geometry

The zone has never been surveyed, so the Eye of Veeshan model is schematic rather than measured. A handful of /loc readings from islands 7 and 8 would fix it. The page says so in place.

Dungeons

Five missing maps

Crushbone, Befallen, Blackburrow, The Hole and The Warrens have survey plates but no navigation map companion. Blackburrow is next; its explicit three-floor structure suits a 3D treatment.

Dungeons

Verification gates, all cleared

All ten plates have passed all three gates, and the evidence for each is recorded on the plates page. That is not the same as complete. It means each plate has been checked against its live source and every coordinate lands somewhere a player can stand — the gaps listed here are what remains.

Help wanted

In-game confirmation

Most of these close with a screenshot or a log line. If you have one, it is worth more than another hour of reading wikis.

NEW Is it still true? — the register of inherited adviceAlmost everyone playing Legends played EverQuest, and arrives carrying twenty-five years of habit. Some of it holds and some was quietly replaced, and the wiki cannot tell you which is which, because large parts of the wiki are the old text. One entry per piece of inherited advice, each with its evidence, its date, and what it would take to settle it. Six to start: multiclass, difficulty not raising mob levels, placeholders in the revamped dungeons, the Per-Level Hunting Guide, underwater weapon types, and whether Paragon of Spirit stacks with Clarity. Three are open, and that is the normal state rather than a failure. The last of those exists because an AI assistant answered the stacking question by citing eqlwiki's Buff Lines page for “slots nine, ten and eleven”. That page describes slots 1–8 and a Layer 2, never mentions Paragon of Spirit, and the spell has no wiki page at all — the URL returns 404. Asked to be more certain, the same tool returned more sources and more specific detail: confidence rose, evidence did not. That pattern is worth more than the spell TypeAdditionDate10 Aug 2026
FIX Najena's spawn percentages, and a T5 number in a structured fieldTwo faults on the flagship plate, both found by an outside reader and both worse than reported. The header carried a 19:00 zone respawn and argued it was current. It is not a zone figure: it comes from the wiki zone page's prose, on a page carrying {{Classic Era}}, so by our own standard it is import text. The infobox on that same page gives the zone spawn timer as 4:50, corrected there on 4 August. The ~19 minute figure is a per-mob cycle and is now labelled as one. Publishing a tier 5 prose number in a structured field and then reasoning about whether it was current is the exact mistake this site exists to catch. Separately, the spawn percentages are almost certainly meaningless. Four sources say the revamped dungeons have no placeholders — the 23 June patch note promising “a striking lack of placeholders”, eqlwiki's Named Mobs category, and Avenrae's own play across ten or more consecutive cycles at The Tenderizer with no placeholder seen, plus hours in Befallen and Blackburrow. Only individual wiki mob pages still describe placeholders, in classic text nobody has revisited. The figures stay printed and struck through: deleting them would hide what the source says. A log across several cycles at one camp settles it outright. Drelzna also gained three drops her wiki record carries and this plate never did — Dark Elf Parts, Bronze Greaves and Tentacle Whip TypeCorrectionDate10 Aug 2026
NEW Tier C, for a first-hand report that is not a logThe source scale ran M, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and a named player saying “I did this last night and it did not work the way the wiki says” fitted none of them. It is not tier M — nothing was parsed, and recollection is not a log. It is not 3 to 5, which are readings of documents. Tier C sits below M and above 3: first-hand, named, dated, unconfirmed, and every claim carrying it publishes who reported it, when, what would confirm it, and that it has not been. A Tier C claim never becomes fact by repetition — it is confirmed and moves, or it stays C visibly. This applies to us as well: our logs are tier M because a parser read them; our memories are not, and exempting ourselves would be the fastest way to corrupt the scale TypeAdditionDate10 Aug 2026
FIX A verification gate that could not be passedGate 3 of the verification standard asked that coordinates be collision-checked against the room list. There is no room list. Across the five zones then held at partial, not one room carried a coordinate and 9 of 209 named mobs named a room or floor at all — so the check had no left-hand side and could not be failed, passed or attempted. Five zones sat waiting on it, which read as work remaining when the truth was that the test did not fit our data. Gate 3 is now the geometry check: every coordinate must land within 120 units of the walkable floor extracted from the game's own mesh files, counted at build time rather than typed. It is the stronger test for whether a position is real — it is how six impossible Najena coordinates were found and withheld — and the weaker one for whether a position matches the room its note names. That trade is stated in the standard rather than buried. Closing the change, The Warrens cleared gate 1: its page was fetched in full for the first time and all 17 named mobs, the 6:40 respawn and the 4–25 level range all agree; and Blackburrow gained the reading key it was the only plate of ten to lack. All ten zones are now fully verified, 176 of 176 coordinates on drawn floor TypeCorrectionDate9 Aug 2026

FIX Six Najena coordinates that sit outside NajenaRathyl, Ekeros, BoneCracker, Officer Grush, Trazdon and A Visiting Priestess are recorded on eqlwiki at positions south of the zone’s own extent, by between 57 and 513 units — outside the dungeon. Najena’s geometry runs from −168 to 546 on the north–south axis, measured from the map file EverQuest Legends installs with the game; two community map sets drawn independently agree to within two units. The east–west value is right in every case and only the north–south value is out, which points at a single column rather than at noise. Our transcription matches the source exactly, so this is the source being wrong rather than us misreading it. The six are withheld from the survey plot and listed with the reason instead. No replacement figure is published, because none is sourced — the official map carries no mob positions, so one /loc reading per mob closes it TypeCorrectionDate8 Aug 2026

Ranges now join the list of mobs that vary by spawn point rather than being placed TypeCorrectionDate8 Aug 2026
