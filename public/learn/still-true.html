EQL Source  /  Learn  /  Is it still true?

You already know
a lot that is wrong.

Almost everyone playing EverQuest Legends played EverQuest, and arrives carrying twenty-five years of habit. Some of it still holds. Some of it was quietly replaced. The wiki cannot tell you which is which, because large parts of the wiki are the old text. This page is the register: one entry per piece of inherited advice, each with its evidence, its date, and what it would take to settle it.

Is haste a percentage, or a flat attack-speed value?

What classic did

A percentage that divides weapon delay. The formula everyone quotes is new delay = delay / (1 + haste), with haste as a decimal, and item tooltips read Haste +41%.

What Legends does

The two best sources in this community disagree, and we were republishing one of them without noticing. EQL Tools states that slow and haste in Legends are flat values on an attack-speed stat — a mob at 100 attack speed with 60 haste sits at 160, and the numbers add rather than compounding. eqlwiki’s own Haste Guide, edited 4 August 2026, still carries the classic percentage formula and cites classic-era raid content around it.

This is not an abstract question for us. Six Plane of Sky reward tooltips on this site carry percentage haste — five of them the identical +41%, which is a copied constant rather than five readings — and they sat inside the tier our tracker labelled verified. They are marked suspect in place now rather than deleted. An outside audit found this on 14 August 2026 and it was right.

The evidence

  • T3EQL Tools Slow, haste & crowd control, read 14 Aug 2026States flat attack-speed values for Legends, with a worked example.
  • T5eqlwiki Haste Guide, read 14 Aug 2026Classic percentage formula, and the surrounding content describes classic-era raid targets. Last edited 4 Aug 2026, which is after launch and does not by itself make the mechanic current.

What would settle it. One screenshot of a Legends haste item tooltip. If it reads a bare number rather than a percentage, EQL Tools is right and every percentage figure on this site is a classic import.

Found by an outside audit of this site, 14 August 2026, which caught it inside our own verified tier before we did.

Do Journeyman’s Boots still come from a quest, or do they drop?

What classic did

On Live in 1999 the boots were made a quest reward on 13 October, and Project 1999 text records that they “formerly dropped from Drelzna in Najena” before that change. That sentence is imported into eqlwiki’s Drelzna page, where it sits inside a structured NPC record and reads like current data.

What Legends does

They drop from Drelzna, and there is no quest. Reported by our collaborator on 14 August 2026: seen dropping for other players and confirmed by guildmates. eqlwiki’s item record agrees, naming Najena and Drelzna as the source.

This is the shape of mistake this register exists for, and an auditor made it in front of us: reading the classic sentence inside the structured NPC page and grading our survey against it. The survey had read the item record and was right. A tier 5 sentence inside a tier 2 container is the most dangerous object in this ecosystem, because it wears the wrong clothes.

The evidence

  • COur collaborator, 14 Aug 2026Has not killed Drelzna personally; has seen the boots drop for others and had it confirmed by guildmates. First-hand but unparsed, so tier C.
  • T2eqlwiki Journeyman’s Boots item recordNames Najena and Drelzna as the source.
  • T5eqlwiki Drelzna NPC pageCarries the Project 1999 sentence about the 1999 quest change, verbatim.

What would settle it. One combat log line showing the loot. We have never killed Drelzna in any session we hold, so this stays tier C until a parse replaces it.

Reported by our collaborator, 14 August 2026.

Does Paragon of Spirit stack with Clarity and bard regeneration songs?

What classic did

On Live EverQuest and on Project 1999 the beastlord line is widely reported to occupy its own buff slots, so it stacks with enchanter Clarity and with bard regeneration songs.

What Legends does

Nobody has published an answer for Legends. Asked on 10 August 2026, an AI assistant answered that the spell uses “slots nine, ten and eleven” and cited eqlwiki’s Buff Lines page. We read that page the same day. It describes slots 1–8 and a Layer 2. It contains no slot 9, 10 or 11, and it does not mention Paragon of Spirit at all. The spell has no page on eqlwiki either — the URL returns 404. The answer was reasoning from an absence, then backfilled from Live and Project 1999.

Asked to be more certain because the answer was worth dozens of hours, the same tool returned more sources and more specific detail. Confidence rose; evidence did not. That is the failure mode every player using an AI assistant will meet, and it is worth more than this one spell.

The evidence

  • T2eqlwiki Buff Lines, read 10 Aug 2026Lists Cantata of Replenishment at +13, Cassindra’s Chorus of Clarity at +7 (+14 pulsed) and Clarity II at +11 in its mana regeneration tables. Slots run 1–8 plus a Layer 2. Paragon of Spirit does not appear.
  • —eqlwiki Paragon of Spirit, read 10 Aug 2026No such page. HTTP 404.

What would settle it. Cast Paragon of Spirit, then have a bard sing Cantata of Replenishment and an enchanter cast Clarity on the same target. A screenshot of the buff bar showing all three at once settles it outright. The combat log adds the timestamps and any overwrite message — if one displaces another, the log shows the displaced spell wearing off within a second of the new cast rather than at its natural duration.

Raised in our own play, 10 August 2026, after an AI assistant gave two confident and differently-worded answers.

Do slashing and blunt weapons still suffer underwater?

What classic did

In classic EverQuest, underwater combat is widely reported to have penalised slashing and blunt weapons, leaving piercing as the sensible choice in water zones. We have not sourced this to a citable record, and say so rather than repeating it as established.

What Legends does

Reported not to apply. A player fighting in Kedge Keep — a zone that is entirely underwater — reports landing normal hits with a sword.

eqlwiki’s Kedge Keep page carries {{Classic Era}}, discusses breathing at length, and says nothing whatever about weapon types underwater. Its search returns zero results for “underwater” on a wiki whose own Kedge Keep page uses the word repeatedly, so the search index is unreliable and an empty result there proves nothing.

The evidence

  • Tier CA player report, 9 August 2026First-hand, in Kedge Keep, using a sword and landing hits. Unconfirmed: a report of play, not a parsed log.
  • T5eqlwiki Kedge Keep, read 9 Aug 2026Classic Era import. Silent on weapon types underwater. Neither confirms nor denies.

What would settle it. One combat log line showing a slashing or blunt weapon landing normal damage on a mob while submerged. If a penalty exists it should show as a visible drop in average damage against the same mob type fought on land.

Credited on the credits page.

Changed

Is a class-locked epic quest closed to everyone else?

What classic did

On Live EverQuest and on Project 1999 a class epic is a class quest. The Fiery Avenger is the paladin epic, and the quest steps as well as the reward are understood to be paladin-only.

What Legends does

The quest is not class-locked. The reward still is. A non-paladin completed every step of the Fiery Avenger, final turn-in included. The item it returns is unchanged: Class: PAL, 2H Slashing, 33/44, Flame Shock combat effect at level 45, Virtuous Bash worn effect.

So the two halves separate. Doing the quest and being able to hold the reward are different permissions, and only the second is still a class gate — which a trio carrying Paladin would clear. This is one epic, on one character. It is a reason to test the other fifteen, not evidence about them: we have no report on any other epic and do not assume this generalises.

The evidence

  • Tier CA player report, 10 August 2026First-hand: “can now confirm can do all parts of the fiery avenger including final turn in as a non paladin”. Reported with an in-game screenshot of the completed item. A report of play, not a parsed log.
  • Tier CItem inspection, 10 August 2026The reward reads Class: PAL, Race: ALL, Lore Equipped, No Trade. The class restriction on the item itself has not changed.

What would settle it. The same run on a second epic with a different class gate would show whether this is the rule or one quest’s wiring. A log of the final turn-in dialogue would raise this from a report to a measurement.

Credited on the credits page. The same player raised the underwater weapon question above.

Does killing a raid boss lock you out for the week?

What classic did

Classic EverQuest has no lockout at all. A boss has a spawn timer, and whoever is standing there when it pops gets the kill.

What Legends does

The lockout is per difficulty, not per boss. The patch notes establish a weekly bonus-loot lockout resetting Tuesdays at 8AM PST, and that a kill made while locked still returns one guaranteed drop from the boss’s unique table. What they do not say is that D0 through D4 each carry their own lockout — so the same boss can be run once per tier.

That reframes a raid week. A first clear at D0 costs nothing at D1 and above, so there is no reason to open at a difficulty you are unsure of. The per-difficulty part is a player report, not a patch note, and the notes neither state it nor contradict it.

The evidence

  • T1Patch notes, 23 June 2026“you will only receive 1 piece of loot per named raid creature that has its own lockout. The raid bonus loot lockouts reset every Tuesday at 8AM PST.”
  • T1Patch notes, 28 July 2026“Killing a raid boss while you have a loot lockout will now give one guaranteed drop from that boss’s unique treasure tables.”
  • Tier CAnnalise (AnnaWulf), 10 August 2026“weekly lock out per difficulty and then daily lock out for 1 loot item till a tues reset”. She also reports a D4 Lord Nagafen taking a long time even with a group, and D1 hit-point pools being a drag solo.

What would settle it. Kill one boss at D0, then open the same boss at D1 and check whether the full loot lockout applies or only the daily one.

Per-difficulty behaviour reported by a player, 10 August 2026. Credited on the credits page.

Can you still plan a character around one class?

What classic did

Every character is one class, chosen at creation and permanent.

What Legends does

No. Characters run three classes at once. Two at creation, a third at level 10, with primary class and race locking permanently at 11. The active trio uses the level of the lowest class in it.

This invalidates a large amount of inherited advice in both directions, and it is the single biggest reason a classic guide can be confidently wrong about Legends.

The evidence

  • T1Official documentation and the level 11 lockPublished behaviour, not inferred. The deity, race and primary class unlock tokens are priced in the Producer’s Letter of 8 July 2026, which only makes sense because those choices lock.

What would settle it. Settled.

Does raising the difficulty just make mobs higher level?

What classic did

No equivalent existed. Difficulty tiers are new.

What Legends does

No. D0–D4 does not raise mob levels at all. It gives mobs player-style class kits, widens aggro ranges and pre-upgrades loot. The tiers are named in game and the zone line prints the name on entry: D0 Base, D1 Awakened, D2 Adaptive, D3 Fused, D4 Refined.

And it starts earlier than published. The published claim is that named mobs run multiclass from D2. In Castle Mistmoore at D1, two ordinary trash types backstabbed 113 times between them — A pledge familiar 62, An initiate familiar 51 while the same types were logged casting Root, Screaming Terror and Shadow Vortex. Backstab is a rogue ability and a spell list is not, so that mob type carried two kits at D1, on trash.

The evidence

  • Tier MOur own logs, Castle Mistmoore, 8 August 2026113 times between them — A pledge familiar 62, An initiate familiar 51, across 7 sessions on 08 Aug 2026, 2 characters, alongside logged casts from the same types. Melee up to 39, backstab up to 168 with per-session averages from 18 to 143.
  • T1Zone line on entryYou have entered The Castle of Mistmoore 1 (Awakened). The number and the name agree, and the modal loot drop of +1 agrees independently.

What would settle it. Settled for D1 and D2, and for the kits: twenty bosses are logged at D3 or D4 with every spell each cast, Cazic-Thule and Innoruuk among them. What is still pinned by nobody is D3 and D4 hit points — damage to kill bounds them from above and no further.

Do named mobs still have placeholders?

What classic did

A named shares its spawn point with a placeholder. You kill the placeholder repeatedly until the named appears, and published spawn percentages describe how often it does.

What Legends does

Gone. The named spawns every cycle. The developer patch note removes placeholders by name from eleven dungeons — ten of the thirteen this site surveys, plus Upper Guk. Plane of Fear, Plane of Hate and Kedge Keep are not on the list, so nothing here is established for them. For the ten that are named, every inherited spawn percentage is historical, and the site prints them struck through rather than deleting them.

We carried this as open while the patch note said it plainly the whole time. The lesson is about where we looked, not about the answer.

The evidence

  • T1Developer patch notes“Removed placeholders from and lowered maximum respawn times in several dungeons: The Hole, Nagafen’s Lair, Lower Guk, Lair of the Splitpaw, The Warrens, Castle Mistmoore, Upper Guk, Crushbone, Befallen, Blackburrow, Najena.”
  • Tier CAvenraeConfirmed in play across these zones before this site existed, and again on 9 August 2026 across ten or more consecutive cycles at one camp.
  • T5eqlwiki individual mob pages, againstDrelzna: “Her placeholder is a necromancer that spawns in front of the chair.” Classic text nobody has revisited. It is wrong.

What would settle it. Settled by the patch note for all eleven named zones. What it does not settle is the second half of its own sentence — maximum respawn times were lowered and no figures were published, so every respawn on this site is a pre-patch ceiling rather than a current value.

Patch note supplied by Avenrae, 10 August 2026.

Is the Per-Level Hunting Guide still good advice?

What classic did

It is one of the most-linked pages on the wiki.

What Legends does

It is a Project 1999 import and it predates everything that matters. It cites P1999 forums, carries the phrase “As of June 2020”, and repeats the P99-specific claim that characters “generally perform like characters three or more levels higher” — a statement about a single-class server at fixed difficulty. It predates both multiclass and D0–D4, so it is wrong in both directions.

The evidence

  • T5eqlwiki Per-Level Hunting GuideProject 1999 import by origin, with its own internal citations to P1999 forums.

What would settle it. Settled.
